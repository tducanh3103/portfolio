An Khang v4.0

Nhà thuốc An Khang | 2026

Problem

Nhà thuốc An Khang had a website but no dedicated app, while its two main competitors, Long Châu and Pharmacity, already had apps. An app was also essential to An Khang's healthcare ecosystem vision (medication reminders, blood pressure tracking, health records, VNeID national digital ID integration), which a website alone could not deliver.

The old system had also become hard to maintain. Hardcoded promotion rules often led to configuration errors and delays, and the interface was due for a refresh after a long time on the same version.

An Khang v4.0 was built to meet both needs: a new app and a new core system to replace the old one.

Role

I led five major areas: the feature list, the roadmap, feature design and specification, the reorganization of category and product pages, and sign-off on the entire new web and app. The most important part of my role was coordination. During design and specification, I distributed features across the team in priority order, so the most critical ones were specified first and the development team could start on them early.

Approach

It started with a "moving house" problem: promotions moved from the ERP to a promotion engine, product content to a product information management system (PIM), and orders and customer data to a sales order management system (SOM). These three are core systems the whole group will share, so every attribute from the old systems had to be mapped and synced into the new ones.

Category and product pages were reorganized so customers could navigate between them more easily. The whole interface was redesigned to current standards in healthcare and pharmacy-cosmetics retail, using a design system I had built and trained the team on beforehand.

Three constraints leading to three decisions

  • Leadership asked for a launch within 1 to 2 months, while tech and PM resources were limited. To meet that deadline, I split the project into two phases, prioritized by business relevance and agreed with all parties in joint meetings. Phase 1 shipped the web and app with the core retail features: listing, cart and promotions. Phase 2 covered news, notifications, product recommendations, and disease and active-ingredient lookup.
  • Keeping SEO strong meant moving the article system onto the new core right away, but doing so would put the schedule at risk. I revisited the trade-off: SEO is not the app's main lever, new downloads are, and web SEO could keep running on the old system in the meantime. The article system therefore moved to phase 2.
  • Specifications and designs had to keep pace with the development team's schedule while staying scalable for later phases. I kept the design system and standard components intact rather than going faster by dropping standards, so the team stayed on schedule and handoffs still met the standard.

After go-live

After phase 1, the SEO system was split into smaller pieces and moved onto the new system step by step, by agreement between PM, SEO and tech. During this period, web traffic did not drop sharply even as overall market traffic fell with the rise of AI search and GEO (optimizing for visibility in AI search engines). The real challenge was running SEO on two systems in parallel, a load that eased only as each piece moved over.

On the app side, downloads were the most urgent priority, so any feature that could drive them went to the front of the queue. For example, when a new feature (a discount voucher on the first 3 orders) was added mid-project, I reviewed the backlog, moved the voucher to the front and pushed back the go-live of disease lookup, which mainly served SEO and had drawn fairly little traffic before.

Lessons

Sales, PM and tech need to meet earlier to agree on the promotion types phase 1 must support. That way, all three teams know up front which ones the new system cannot support, instead of finding out after go-live.

Results

In the first month after go-live, the platform brought in VND 5 billion in online revenue (web and app combined), with an average of 73K daily active users on the app. Three months after go-live, the app reached 105K cumulative downloads.

On the system side, the new core is more stable and lighter so pages load faster. Promotion time-to-market is also shorter than on the old system thanks to removing hardcoded rules and standardizing promotion types and the setup process.
