An Khang v4.0: from a website to a web and app ecosystem on a new core system

Problem

An Khang had only a website version and no dedicated app, while its two direct competitors, Long Châu and Pharmacity, both already had apps. An app was also a must-have for the health-care ecosystem vision for An Khang (medication reminders, blood pressure tracking, health records, VNeID integration), which a website alone could not deliver. In addition, the old system had become cumbersome: hardcoded promotion rules easily led to configuration errors and delays, and the interface needed a refresh after staying on the old version for so long. These two needs set the stage for An Khang v4.0: building a new app and replacing the core system.

Role

I led five major areas: the feature list, the roadmap, feature design and specification, the re-architecture of product listing pages, and sign-off on the entire new web and app. The most important part of my role was coordination: allocating features across the team during design and specification, so that the most critical features went in first and the Dev team could prioritize them.

Approach

It started with a "moving house" problem: promotions moved from the ERP to a Promotion Engine, product content to PIM, orders and customer data to SOM. These three are core systems the whole group will share later, so every attribute from the old systems had to match and sync into the new ones. Listing and detail pages were reorganized so customers could navigate more easily between category pages and product pages. The whole interface was redesigned to market standards, on a design system I had built and trained the team on beforehand. Designs were then sequenced from highest to lowest priority, so that Dev could stay on schedule with the most important features.

Three constraints leading to three decisions

  • Leadership asked for a launch within 1 to 2 months, while tech and PM resources were limited. To meet this, I split the project into two phases, chosen by business relevance and agreed in joint meetings between all parties involved. Phase 1 shipped web and app together with the core retail features: listing, cart and promotions. Phase 2 held news, notifications, product recommendations, and disease and active-ingredient lookup.
  • Keeping SEO at its best meant moving the article system onto the new core right away, but doing so would put the schedule at risk. I weighed it again: for the app, SEO is not the main lever, new downloads are, and web SEO could still run on the old system. The article system therefore moved to Phase 2.
  • Specifications and designs had to keep pace with the Dev team's schedule while still scaling later. I kept the design system and standard components intact instead of going faster by dropping standards, so the team stayed on schedule and still handed off to standard.

After go-live

The SEO system was split into smaller pieces and moved into the new system step by step after Phase 1. During this period, web traffic did not drop sharply even as overall market traffic fell with the rise of AI and GEO (optimizing for visibility in AI search engines). The harder part was running SEO on two systems in parallel, a load that eased only as each piece moved over. On the app side, the goal was downloads. When a feature to drive installs came in, a discount voucher on the first 3 orders, I reviewed the backlog and pushed back the go-live of disease lookup, since the feature mainly served SEO and its past traffic was fairly low.

Lessons

Sales, PM and IT need to meet earlier to settle the promotion concepts that Phase 1 must support, so that all three sides see right away which promotion types the system cannot handle, instead of finding out after go-live.

Results

In the first month after launch: VND 5 billion in online revenue (web and app combined) and 73K average daily active users on the app. Three months after go-live: 105K cumulative downloads. Page load time and the time to put a promotion live were both shorter than on the old system, thanks to removing hardcoded rules and standardizing promotion types and the setup process.
