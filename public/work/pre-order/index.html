An Khang Pharmacy | 2024 | Product Manager

Pre-Order for cancer and rare medications: building an entirely new sales channel

Problem

In Vietnam, patients with cancer or rare diseases struggle to find their medication, and often have to buy through wholesale channels of unknown origin. An Khang wanted to solve this by opening a legitimate channel that gives customers access to medication from a clearly identified brand, with a trustworthy origin and transparent pricing. The project reached me as nothing more than a business idea.

Role

I defined the entire flow and coordinated Sales, Customer Service, Logistics and Dev. I personally handled the research and UI design, wrote the feature specifications, and ran the kickoff meetings and the post go-live review meetings.

Approach

I started by listening: interviewing the sales team and patients, and studying competitors to understand how the market runs this model. The biggest obstacle was that the business did not want to hold stock, given the high value of the drugs and the risk of inventory sitting unsold for a long time. Many options went on the table, such as ordering directly with no consultation, or with no deposit. All were weighed against operational reality until the team agreed on the best operating flow: View the product page > Fill in the form > Consultation call from staff > 100% deposit > Source the medication and deliver.

Three constraints leading to three design decisions

  • As a prescription drug with a price that changes over time, the product page does not list a price. Staff quote the price directly on the consultation call.
  • With no inventory, delivery time depends on the supplier and cannot be fixed with a firm commitment.
  • Filling in a form and paying a deposit is an entirely new way for customers to buy, so the guidance had to be clear and appear right when users look up the product.

As a result, this is a product page unlike any ordinary medication page. I tried, in turn, adding the medication to the cart like any other product, then placing the form directly on the page, and finally settled on a registration form with the full UI flow: from filling in the form, to a confirmation that the request was sent, to the list of registrations for the same medication. The guidance in particular had to be easy to spot without adding bulk to a prescription page already full of information.

After go-live

I listened back to consultation calls and realized customers were surprised when they had to pay a 100% deposit on the order value, or unhappy that delivery took too long. I put the 100% deposit information and delivery notes on the page, and worked with the consulting team to shorten callback time for customers, against a target of responding within 48 hours.

Lessons

  • Listening back to consultation calls is a very good source of insight for improving the UI and operations of a new feature.
  • Launch is not the end. Proactively monitoring operations and meeting with the relevant teams to improve the process helps a feature mature and truly create value.

Results

In the first month, although customers submitted order forms, no order was placed with a successful deposit. From month two, orders began to pour in, and the category still runs steadily, contributing consistent and growing revenue. Response time dropped from one week to about 24 hours, with the fastest orders answered within 2 hours against a commitment of 48 hours. The average order value is tens of times higher than a regular order.
