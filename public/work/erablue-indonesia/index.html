Mobile World Group | 2022 | Product Manager

Erablue Indonesia: laying the first brick in a new market

Problem

MWG wanted to expand its home appliance and electronics retail across Southeast Asia, and Indonesia was the chosen market. At the time, the assessment was that the market ran about 2 to 3 years behind Vietnam, had a large population, had no retailer on the scale of Điện Máy Xanh, and had no strong online sales service with delivery and installation. Phase 1 of Erablue was a website without a dedicated app, showing products and the stores that had them in stock. Its core goal was to bring customers into the stores.

Role

I was part of the PM research team, consolidated the findings with the team, and delivered the final research presentation. I then took on all of the UX for the project: building the information architecture, the design system and the interfaces for the agreed features, and accepting the build once development finished. Translation and product content were handled by another team.

Approach

We studied the Indonesian market and its retailers, including well-known names such as Tokopedia, Electronic City, My Hartono and Eraspace. We compared features and promotion concepts against Điện Máy Xanh across three stages: search, hesitation, ordering. From that we proposed a feature list by phase, then reviewed it with IT and the Business team to settle the scope of phase 1.

Three key decisions

  1. Trim the feature list to match the goal of driving store visits. The original plan included a cart and online purchase, but warehousing and logistics were not ready, so the Business team decided phase 1 would not include that flow. Features such as product comparison, filters and comments were also cut so that development could hit the go-live date. Design worked closely with the developers to keep only what served the business goal.
  2. Keep the overall Điện Máy Xanh structure, adjust the details. The website structure of the two markets was not very different, which made entering Indonesia easier. The IA was therefore inherited, and the effort went into small details.
  3. Learn user habits from local sites. While browsing competitor sites, I noticed that Indonesian retailers highlighted discount percentages far more than in Vietnam. On product cards, the plain percentage became a label with a background, placed ahead of the original price. Vertical banners were added, which suit the vertical rectangular product cards and keep the page cohesive. The color palette was also broader and brighter than the Vietnamese version. Users were already used to how other retailers presented things, so these small details made a big difference.

Challenge

The biggest challenge was hitting the go-live date. IT and PM agreed early on a handover date for all designs and specifications, and design ran to that date. After handover, I worked with the developers on edge cases as they came up.

Lessons

This was my first project for a market outside Vietnam. For a product to serve users well at launch, you need to understand the culture, how the market operates and what its users care about. The only way is to invest heavily in research before getting into the details, and that was what I learned most from this project.

Results

The website and the offline stores launched together on time in Indonesia in 2022, a milestone for the whole PM and IT team.
