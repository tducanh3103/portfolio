Erablue Indonesia

Mobile World Group | 2022

Problem

Mobile World Group (MWG) wanted to expand its home appliance and electronics retail across Southeast Asia, and Indonesia was the chosen market. At the time, the assessment was that Indonesia was about 2 to 3 years behind Vietnam, with a large population yet neither a retailer on the scale of Điện Máy Xanh (MWG's home appliance chain in Vietnam) nor a strong online sales service with advanced delivery and installation.

Phase 1 of Erablue was a website showing product availability by store, with the core goal of bringing customers into the stores.

Role

As part of the PM research team, I consolidated the findings and delivered the final research presentation. I then owned the UX for phase 1: adapting the information architecture from Điện Máy Xanh, designing the design system and the interfaces for the agreed features, and accepting the build once development finished. Translation and product content were handled by a separate team.

Approach

We studied the Indonesian market and its retailers, including Tokopedia, Electronic City, My Hartono and Eraspace. We compared their features and promotion concepts with those of Điện Máy Xanh across three stages of the customer journey: search, hesitation and ordering. From there we proposed a feature list by phase, then reviewed it with tech and the business team to settle the scope of phase 1.

Three key decisions

  1. Trim the feature list to match the goal of driving store visits. The original plan included a cart and online purchase, but warehousing and logistics were not ready, so the business team decided phase 1 would leave out online purchase. Product comparison, filters and reviews were also cut to keep development on schedule for go-live. I worked closely with the development team to keep only what served the business goal.
  2. Keep the overall Điện Máy Xanh structure and adjust the details. Electronics retail websites in Vietnam and Indonesia had similar structures, which made entering Indonesia easier. That's also why I adapted the information architecture from Điện Máy Xanh and put the effort into the details that needed to change.
  3. Learn user habits from local sites. While browsing competitor sites, I noticed that Indonesian retailers highlighted discount percentages far more prominently than Vietnamese ones. To apply that, I turned the plain percentage on product cards into a label with a background, placed before the original price to make discounts stand out. I also added vertical banners that suit the portrait product cards, bringing more cohesion to the page and deliberately varying its patterns to break up a monotonous layout. On top of that, the color palette became broader and brighter than on Điện Máy Xanh to bring in the flavor of Indonesian market preferences.

The reasoning behind these choices is Jakob's Law: users spend most of their time on other sites, so they expect a new site to work like the ones they already know. Indonesian users were already used to how local retailers presented promotions, so following those patterns made the site feel familiar to them, and these small details made a big difference.

Challenge

The biggest challenge was meeting the go-live date. Tech and PM agreed early on a handover date for all designs and specifications, and I delivered everything by that date. After handover, I kept working closely with the development team to resolve edge cases as they came up.

Lessons

This was my first project for a market outside Vietnam. For a product to serve users well at launch, a team needs to understand the culture, how the market operates and what its users care about. The most reliable way to get there is to research heavily before settling the details.

Results

In 2022, against a tight deadline, the website and the offline stores opened together in Indonesia on the planned day, and leadership recognized it as a major success for the whole team.
